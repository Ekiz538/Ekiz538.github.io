Project 03 · Industry internship

Veeam Data Protection Research

A sales-track research project at Mitsumi Distribution across East and West Africa — mapping Veeam's backup and recovery portfolio, licensing and the wider vendor line card.

Veeam portfolio Back to projects

Sales · Veeam · Technical research · Sep 2026 – present · Kampala, Uganda

The context

Mitsumi Distribution — operating in Uganda through Devjyot Traders — carries an enterprise portfolio spanning data protection, cybersecurity, networking and virtualisation. As a sales intern my focus is Veeam, the backup and data-protection line. The job is to understand the portfolio well enough to have a useful conversation, not just repeat product names.

The research question

Veeam's value only becomes concrete when a customer connects it to their own risk: how much data can they afford to lose, how fast must they recover, and would their backups survive an attacker who already has admin credentials? Most outreach opens with product names instead. My focus is to start with business continuity, then map Veeam's portfolio onto it.

What I'm working on

  • Sales conversations. Learning how to frame Veeam around business risk — downtime, recovery time, data loss and cyber resilience — before talking about individual products.
  • Licensing and recovery research. Building a working reference for VUL licensing, backup fundamentals and VMware/Broadcom changes so the conversations stay accurate.
  • The vendor line card. Mapping where Veeam, Commvault, VMware, Barracuda, Fortinet and the other vendors fit in a customer environment.
  • Customer-facing messaging. Turning that research into clear, defensible language for partners and customers, without overstating what has been delivered.

The research behind it

Sales messaging is only as good as the facts under it. I'm building a working reference across the portfolio so the claims and conversations stay accurate:

  • Backup fundamentals — RPO versus RTO, replication versus backup, the 3-2-1-1-0 rule, immutability and air-gapping, and where full, incremental and differential backups trade off.
  • Veeam licensing — the VUL instance model, edition capabilities, what consumes a licence and what does not, and how standby/DR replicas are treated.
  • VMware/Broadcom changes — per-core subscription licensing with the 16-core-per-CPU floor, VCF 9 licensing through VCF Operations, and vSAN capacity arithmetic.
  • The vendor line card — Barracuda, Fortinet, Array Networks, Symantec/Broadcom, Ivanti, BlackBerry, Veeam, Commvault and VMware: what each does, and where it sits in a customer environment.

Where it stands

The output so far is a clearer sales-ready understanding of the Veeam portfolio: one customer risk, one mapped capability, one next step. The research is still in progress and has not been shipped as a finished deliverable. It is also building the channel fluency that sales work demands — how an OEM, distributor, reseller and end customer relate, and where a distributor genuinely adds value beyond price.

Honest constraints

Product claims and licensing terms change, so the research is re-verified against current vendor documentation. Availability varies by region, and no customer-specific data is used. Knowing where a claim stops being defensible is part of the work.
